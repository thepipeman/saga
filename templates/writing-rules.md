# Writing rules

Apply these rules to the prose in every document you write. Do not apply them to code, identifiers, Mermaid, tables of values, or quoted text.

## Sentences

- Write one idea in each sentence.
- Keep a sentence to 25 words or fewer. Keep a step or instruction to 20 words or fewer.
- Use active voice. Name the actor: "CCAP rejects the request", not "the request is rejected".
- Use simple tenses. Use present perfect only when it says that a result is still true now.
- Do not use semicolons. Do not join two ideas with an em dash. Write two sentences.
- Write each step, condition, or option as a list item when there are 3 or more.

## Words

- Use the single verb, not a phrasal verb: "start", not "spin up". "Remove", not "take out".
- Use the verb, not a noun form of it: "validate", not "perform validation of".
- Use one name for one thing in the whole document. Do not rotate synonyms.
- Stack no more than 3 nouns: "push token lookup", not "device push token lookup result".
- Do not use marketing adjectives (seamless, robust, powerful). Give the fact or the number.
- Keep the subject, verb, and article. Do not drop words to save space.

## Meaning

- Keep every hedge. "May fail" stays "may fail". Do not turn it into "fails".
- Add no fact, cause, or number that the source does not give.
- If a shorter sentence loses a condition, a scope limit, or a number, keep the longer sentence.
